## What the sources say, per artist

- **Phuture, Acid Tracks (Trax, 1987).** TB-303 plus TR-707 and TR-727 drum machines; Marshall Jefferson produced. [S: Wikipedia, Roland article]. Accounts differ on how the 303 was acquired (pawn shop, about $40, vs bought after hearing a demo). The drums are therefore NOT 808 or 909: a 707/727 is a sample-based box, so the DAW needs the sampler for them.
- **Plastikman, Sheet One / Spastik (1993).** TB-303 central. Sources disagree on drums: one says Spastik is mostly TR-808, another 808 and 909 together; a photo caption lists two 303s, a TR-909 and an MC-202; SH-101 is also named for Hawtin. [S: GearNews, Equipboard, search summary]. No verified per-track gear list. Treat "808 plus 909 plus 303" as the working guess for Spastik. "Gak" is documented only as a TB-303 pattern name (Bank 1, 3A, May 1993) [S, weak source].
- **Robert Hood, Minimal Nation (Axis, 1994).** Hood's own account (via Red Bull Music Academy): SH-101, Juno 2, TR-909 (bought from Gerald Donald), Yamaha DX100 borrowed from Jeff Mills, Yamaha QY sequencer, four-channel mixer. The album began with a Juno 2 chord sound plus one pattern. [S: search summaries of RBMA, Wikipedia, GearNews, DJ Mag]. Wikipedia and RA say Juno 2; the Wikipedia link points to the Alpha Juno, so confirm. Best-documented minimal record found.
- **Jeff Mills, The Bells (Purpose Maker, made 1994, released spring 1996).** One review says the track develops from a single percussive idea with the TR-909 as its backbone [S: reviews via search]. Studio gear for this record is NOT confirmed. Mills' later live rigs (Korg MS2000R etc.) are not evidence for 1994.
- **Basic Channel / Maurizio.** "Notoriously tight-lipped"; consensus guess of a Prophet-5 [S: search summary of a gear article]. Chords are described as hollow, with dub delay, noise and crackle over the rhythm [S: RA, Attack]. I found no documented drum machine. Quadrant Dub is BC-06, 1994; M-Series 1997, singles "Ploy" 1992 to "M-7" 1997 [S: Wikipedia].
- **Daft Punk, Homework (1997).** The duo's own gear list, via a Japanese magazine interview: Juno-106, MC-202, MKS-80, TB-303, Prophet-VS; TR-707, TR-808, TR-909 [S: Mixmag, MusicTech]. Per-track gear is not given in what I saw. Many Homework tracks are sample-based, so the "one synth plus one box" claim does not hold for all of them.
- **Green Velvet / Cajmere.** Early tracks on a roughly $60 keyboard, a cheap four-track and a cheap drum machine; a synth riff, drum machine rhythm and creeped-out vocal [S: Line Noise]. Flash is on Relief, 1995 [S]. Models NOT identified.
- **Villalobos, Dexter (Alcachofa, Playhouse, 2003).** Attack describes an electro-style drum-machine closed hat in eighths, micro-percussion and a variable-pitch open hat; no hardware named [S].

## The five to start with

1. **Robert Hood, Minimal Nation.** Best evidence in the whole list (Hood's own gear list), two main instruments, and the drum box is a real 909, which is exactly Batch 2's target. The Juno 2 chord stab asks for a poly analog patch with chorus and nothing more.
2. **Plastikman, Gak.** One 303 line over a thin beat; tests the mono acid synth and its filter and accent behaviour, and gives Batch 3 a clean "mono analog" case. Verify drum model by ear before trusting any label.
3. **Phuture, Acid Tracks.** The origin of the sound, and the most useful test of the sampler: the drums are 707/727 samples, not 808/909, so the classifier must say "sample" and the DAW must load a sampler program. Document the 303 sweeps as filter automation.
4. **Jeff Mills, The Bells.** Pairs a 909 with one tuned, delayed melodic synth; tests 909 programming (the accent and the way Mills plays the transport) and a delay tail. Gear for the melody is unconfirmed, so Ear Crew measurements will drive the synth patch.
5. **Basic Channel, Phylyps Trak.** Difficulty 4 and no documented gear, but it is the cleanest test of effects: stab chord into long dub delay, with noise and crackle. It exercises Batch 3's "reverb present, built-in or outboard" rule. Start it after the other four are working.

Not in my five: Daft Punk (layered, sample-heavy, many instruments) and Villalobos (micro-percussion, five or more voices).
