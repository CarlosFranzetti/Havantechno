# N1 Naming researcher: transcript

Date: 2026-11-27. Role: naming researcher. Searches: 14 WebSearch calls (standard mode), one per candidate. Firecrawl not used.

Read this first: I cannot check trademarks or domain availability reliably, and I did not try. Every clash finding below comes from a web search summary (tagged SNIPPET). I opened no pages, so nothing is tagged READ. "Nothing found in 1 search" is not proof that a name is free. One search per name is thin; a second query with different wording could turn up a product the first missed.

## 1. Thirty candidates

### (a) Dub and soundsystem vocabulary
1. Version. Dub term for a reworked B-side; the app literally makes a version of a record. Collides with the everyday software word "version".
2. Dubplate. A one-off acetate cut for a soundsystem; the owner's first-round idea.
3. Rewind. The DJ call to pull a tune back; also the A/B toggle against the original.
4. Selector. The person who picks the records; the app picks which instrument made which part.
5. Havana Dub. Ties to the company's roots (Havant, Havana); a long two-word name.
6. HVT. The current house mark; already the model prefix (HVT-303).
7. Riddim. Jamaican word for the instrumental backing track; exactly what the app rebuilds.
8. Dubwise. "In the manner of dub"; adjective turned product name.
9. Soundclash. Soundsystems competing; the A/B against the original is a clash of two versions.
10. Stepper. Stepper rhythms in dub and techno; also a step sequencer.
11. Siren. The dub siren; short and loud, and it is an instrument the app could ship.
12. Skank. The offbeat guitar chop; short but a rude word in some contexts. Likely drop.

### (b) What the app does (take apart, rebuild, copy, trace)
13. Recut. Cut the record again; a recut is also a reworked edit.
14. Teardown. Hardware term for taking a product apart to see how it is made.
15. Facsimile. An exact copy; honest about the goal of the rebuild.
16. Retrace. Go back over the lines; stem separation followed by transcription.
17. Unpick. Take out the stitching to see how it was sewn; British and a little homely.
18. Stemma. Manuscript scholars' family tree of copies; the lineage of a record's parts.
19. Tracer. Follows a signal back to its source; common word.
20. Reissue. A record put out again; the rebuilt project is a reissue of the original.

### (c) Studio and record-cutting vocabulary
21. Acetate. The soft disc a lathe cuts for a test.
22. Lacquer. The coated aluminium disc a cutting lathe cuts.
23. Stamper. The nickel plate that presses the vinyl; the thing that makes copies.
24. Matrix. The number scratched into the run-out of a record; also a catalogue term.
25. Testpress. The test pressing sent for approval before the run.
26. Lathe. The cutting machine; short, plain, a physical object.
27. Mother. The plated negative between master and stamper (a "mother" is a real step in the process).

### (d) Coined or compound words, short and pronounceable in English and Spanish
28. Trazo. Spanish for stroke or trace; short, two syllables, reads the same in both languages.
29. Replika. Respelling of replica; looks like a typo and likely collides with a known AI app (not checked).
30. Dubcopy. Plain compound: dub and copy. Weak, generic.

The nine first-round names are numbers 1 to 6, 13, 14, 15 (Version, Dubplate, Rewind, Selector, Havana Dub, HVT, Recut, Teardown, Facsimile).

## 2. Clash checks (14 names)

Each line: what the search summary reported. All SNIPPET unless stated.

1. Dubplate (query: "Dubplate" audio software). SNIPPET: Airwindows DubPlate, a mastering-style plugin (CLAP, AU, VST3, LV2 and standalone), described by its maker as emulating a lacquer dubplate; a follow-up DubPlate2 page exists. Direct clash in the same industry, in the same spelling. Worst of the set.
2. Recut (query: "Recut" DAW plugin). Nothing found in 1 search. The results mentioned ReCycle (Reason Studios, a loop slicer) and a plugin called Rewind, neither the same. From general knowledge, not checked: "recut" is a film and video editing term, so expect noise in search results.
3. Teardown (query: "Teardown" audio software music). SNIPPET: the video game Teardown by Tuxedo Labs (a voxel destruction game on Steam), plus a sound designer's page about that game's audio. No audio software of that name found. The game is well known, so searches and store listings would be crowded.
4. Facsimile (query: "Facsimile" audio software DAW plugin). Nothing found in 1 search.
5. Acetate (query: "Acetate" audio software DAW plugin). SNIPPET: Acetate, a standalone desktop mastering app aimed at techno and house producers and DJs (listing seen on AlternativeTo; one source only, maker not named in the summary, price given as $63). Same audience and same genre as this app. Serious clash.
6. Lacquer (query: "Lacquer" audio software DAW plugin). SNIPPET: Insert Lacquer by Insert Audio, a vinyl-style saturation plugin (AU, VST3, AAX) listed on KVR, version 0.1.4. Also SimuLathe from Tokyo Dawn Labs (a lathe-cutting emulation) surfaced as a related product, not the same name. A small plugin, but a direct same-name hit.
7. Stamper (query: "Stamper" audio software music plugin). Nothing found in 1 search. Near misses: AmpStamp (an iPhone app by The Musicology Group) and Stagecraft Software's plugin catalogue, none named Stamper.
8. Matrix (query: "Matrix" audio software DAW plugin music). SNIPPET: Matrix by Mainstream Audio (a Windows VST synth), Sound Particles AudioMatrix (a routing utility), and Samplitude's "Matrix" modes and routing matrix. A very common word with several hits.
9. Retrace (query: "Retrace" audio software music stem separation). Nothing found in 1 search. The results were all other stem tools (Audionamix XTRAX Stems, Ableton Live stem separation, Mixcraft, StemRoller and others).
10. Trazo (query: "Trazo" audio software music). Nothing found in 1 search.
11. Stemma (query: "Stemma" audio software music stems). Nothing found in 1 search. Many stem tools appeared (StemRoller, StemDeck, Stemgen, Native Instruments Stem Creator, LANDR Stems, Serato Sample) with "Stem-" names, so the word sits in a crowded neighbourhood.
12. Dubwise (query: "Dubwise" audio software plugin). Nothing found in 1 search. Near misses: Dub Filter (AudioThing), Dubler 2 (Vochlea), DubMaster.
13. Selector (query: "Selector" audio software DAW music app). Nothing found in 1 search, but the word is generic and the search is too blunt to say much; it returned only general DAW pages.
14. Rewind (query: "Rewind" audio software DAW plugin). SNIPPET: Rewind by MonkeyC (a retrospective capture plugin, AU, VST3, AAX and standalone), Rewind Professional by Fourier Audio (a macOS live sound app, marked legacy), and Rewind by MeldaProduction (an instrument). Three products already carry the name.

Not searched: Version, HVT, Havana Dub. Version is the most generic word in software; I would expect it to be unsearchable and unprotectable. I did not check it.

Summary of how bad the common words are: Rewind has three audio products; Matrix has at least three; Dubplate, Lacquer and Acetate each have one in the same trade (Acetate even targets techno producers). Teardown has a famous game. The rarer or foreign words (Trazo, Stemma, Recut, Facsimile, Retrace, Stamper, Dubwise) returned nothing in one search, which means little for Recut and Retrace (common English words) and more for Trazo and Stemma.

## 3. Shortlist of 5, ranked

Naming rule used below: model prefix is three letters plus the existing number (today HVT-303); the extension is the project file.

### 1. Recut
- Reason: says what the app does (cut the record again), is dub-adjacent (a recut is a version), and is short and easy to say in Spanish.
- Clash: nothing found in 1 search. Worst risk is not software but search noise from the film and video sense of "recut" (general knowledge, not checked).
- Extension: `.recut`. Prefix: `RCT-303`, `RCT-808`, `RCT-106`.
- Weakness: a verb, so hard to search for alone; and "cut" suggests editing audio, not instrument modelling.

### 2. Stemma
- Reason: a stemma is the family tree of copies of a text, so it fits tracing a record back to its parts, and it is close to "stem" without being a stem-tool name.
- Clash: nothing found in 1 search, but it sits among many "Stem-" products and could be mistaken for one of them.
- Extension: `.stemma`. Prefix: `STM-303`, `STM-808`.
- Weakness: obscure to most people; some will read it as "stem" plus a suffix. Pronunciation varies (STEM-a or STEHM-a).

### 3. Facsimile
- Reason: an exact copy, honest about the aim, and the A/B against the original is a facsimile test.
- Clash: nothing found in 1 search.
- Extension: `.fsm` or `.facsimile`; `.fax` would be wrong (it is a known fax file type). Prefix: `FCS-303`, or `FSM-303`.
- Weakness: long (four syllables, ten letters); the word suggests fax machines; it promises a perfect copy the app cannot deliver, so it invites complaint.

### 4. Trazo
- Reason: Spanish for trace or stroke, two syllables, the same in English and Spanish, rare as a software name.
- Clash: nothing found in 1 search.
- Extension: `.trazo`. Prefix: `TRZ-303`, `TRZ-808`.
- Weakness: English speakers will not know it means anything; it says nothing about dub or records.

### 5. Retrace
- Reason: go back over the lines of a finished record, which is what separation and transcription do.
- Clash: nothing found in 1 search, but it is a common verb and I expect others to use it (not checked).
- Extension: `.retrace`. Prefix: `RTR-303`.
- Weakness: generic, hard to search for, and implies a path you walk again rather than a thing you build.

Close runner-up: Dubwise. It keeps the dub link and found nothing in 1 search, but it is the most genre-locked name and the name of the file (`.dubwise`) is long. Prefix would be `DBW-303`.

### First-round names I would drop
- Dubplate: same name as a shipping Airwindows plugin, in the same trade.
- Rewind: three audio products already use it.
- Selector: too generic to find, and the search showed nothing useful either way.
- Version: the most generic word in software, impossible to search for and clashes with every "version" label in the app itself (file versions, app versions).
- Teardown: a famous game with the same name; also reads as hardware repair.
- HVT: keep as the company mark and prefix, not as the app's name (three letters, no meaning to anyone outside).
- Havana Dub: keep as a label or crew name, too long to be the app's name.
- Keep in play: Recut and Facsimile.

Also drop from my own list: Acetate and Lacquer (direct same-trade clashes), Matrix (crowded), Skank (reads badly), Replika (likely clash, unchecked).

## 4. What I could not check
- Trademarks: no registry searched (USPTO, EUIPO, UKIPO or others). A name with no software hit can still be registered in class 9 or class 41.
- Domains and social handles: not checked. Do not assume .com, .app, a GitHub organisation or a package name is free.
- Mobile app stores, Steam and package registries (npm, PyPI, crates.io): not searched.
- Web search covers only what the search engine returned; it is US-only and returned summaries, not pages. I did not open any page, so every finding is a SNIPPET and may be wrong or out of date (for example, Acetate's maker and date are unknown to me).
- One query per name. A name with "nothing found" could still have a product the query missed, in particular Recut, Retrace and Facsimile, which are common words.
- Meanings in other languages beyond Spanish and English: not checked (Stemma, Trazo and Recut may mean something rude or odd elsewhere).
- Version, HVT, Havana Dub, Riddim, Siren, Stepper, Lathe, Mother, Tracer, Unpick, Reissue, Testpress, Soundclash, Dubcopy, Replika: no clash search at all.
- Pronunciation in Spanish: my judgement, not tested with speakers.
