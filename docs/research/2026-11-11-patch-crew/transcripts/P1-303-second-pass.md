## 1. Item by item

### 1.1 Filter: pole count, real slope

- States: "these filters are 4th-order, 4-pole, 24dB/octave attenuation low-pass filters, plain and simple" and gives the TB-303 core transfer function, with the lowest capacitor halved: `H_tb(s) = -1 / (s^4 + 6.727 s^3 + 14.142 s^2 + 9.514 s + 1)` (normalised to the cutoff `ω_c`), poles at (-3.24), (-2.33), (-1.04), (-0.13). Source: Stinchcombe, `https://www.timstinchcombe.co.uk/index.php?pge=diode`, section "The 18dB versus 24dB 'dispute'" and the derivation above it. He also reports a SPICE run of the core: stage gradients -6.1, -11.9, -17.9, -24.1 dB/oct between 61 kHz and 122 kHz (table on the same page). Tag: SCHEMATIC + SPICE (his, on a simplified circuit); not a measurement of a unit.
- Why people hear "18 dB": he says the transition "starts earlier, and lasts longer than ... the Moog transistor ladder", so it "behav[es] more like 18dB/octave ... in the main region in which they are used". MY-CALC from his polynomial: relative gain at 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 times the cutoff = -21.9, -34.4, -51.9, -73.3, -96.6 dB, i.e. 12.5, 17.5, 21.4, 23.3 dB per octave in successive octaves. So the real slope near the knee is about 12 to 18 dB/oct and only reaches 24 asymptotically.
- Spec 1 said: 4 poles, 24 dB nominal, 14/19/22 dB per octave from my port of Open303. Agreed; no change to the pole count. Build: the processor ports Open303's coupled ladder (4 poles). No change.
- Other changes to the core: halving the lowest capacitor "is closely equivalent to shifting the whole response of the filter up or down in frequency" by 2^0.25 = 1.189 (Stinchcombe, same page). No sound change: it is only a cutoff calibration.

### 1.2 Resonance: critical gain, whether a real unit self-oscillates

- States (stock unit): Wikipedia's infobox says "24 dB/oct low-pass resonant filter, non-self-oscillating" (SECONDARY, `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_TB-303`, infobox). The Devil Fish manual lists, among its changes, "Filter can be switched to self resonance" and says "The range of the Resonance control can be switched to double the usual feedback so as to allow the filter to self-oscillate at mid and high frequencies" (SECONDARY, Whittle, Devil Fish Manual, "New and Changed Controls"; he built and repaired the units, but this is a statement, not a measurement).
- MY-CALC from Stinchcombe's `H_tb` (ideal core, feedback of gain G around the four stages, no coupling capacitors): Routh-Hurwitz on `s^4 + 6.727 s^3 + 14.142 s^2 + 9.514 s + (1 + G)` gives the stability limit `G = 17.0`, with the oscillation frequency at `1.189 ω_c` (= 2^0.25 ω_c). Open303's 17 is therefore the diode ladder's critical gain (the transistor ladder's is 4). Closed-loop response `1/(D(s) + G)`: DC gain falls as `1/(1+G)` (-14.0, -19.6, -22.3, -24.6 dB at G = 4, 8.5, 12, 16), and the peak over the DC level is +1.5, +7.8, +14.0, +19.3 (G = 14), +29.5 (G = 16), +49.7 dB (G = 16.9).
- Reading the two together (MY-CALC, an inference): if doubling the stock maximum feedback allows oscillation "at mid and high frequencies" but not at low ones, the stock maximum core gain lies between 8.5 and 17. Low frequencies are excluded because the coupling highpasses (1.3) remove loop gain there. So a stock 303 at full resonance has a high but finite peak (+8 to +29 dB over its own DC gain at a mid cutoff, by the table above) and does not hold a sine.
- Spec 1 said: `k = 17 r`, i.e. critical at r = 1 for the ideal core; "peak +5.1 dB at 1 kHz, +14.1 dB at 3 kHz" (my port, relative to unit input). P5 measured "no self-oscillation at resonance 1" and a peak of "up to 7.3 dB over the half-frequency harmonic at 0.95".
- Change for the build (`resonance` internal mapping, no new parameter): keep `resonance` 0 to 1 and the skew `r = (1 - exp(-3x)) / (1 - exp(-3))`, but set the maximum core gain `k_max` to a value in the bracket 8.5 to 17 instead of exactly 17. The upper end of the bracket (about 14 to 16) gives the stronger, more vocal peak the owner will expect from acid; the lower end (about 9) gives a polite one. The bracket is derived; the choice inside it is a design choice (basis: prior). My recommendation to try first: `k_max` = 15 (peak about +25 dB over DC at the ideal core), then let the 150 Hz-class feedback highpass reduce it at low cutoffs, as it already does. P5's bench should report the peak above the passband level, not above the half-frequency harmonic, so the numbers compare with the table in this section. Confidence: bracket high, point inside it low.

### 1.3 Coupling highpasses around the filter

- States (derived, SPICE on his model of the real circuit): "the high-pass effects that all the coupling caps around the feedback loop introduce ... turn the transfer function into a nine-pole monstrosity, with five new zeroes ... The five new poles are all clustered at a low frequency, around 10Hz or so ... they create another resonant peak at this frequency" (Stinchcombe, `...?pge=diode`, update 19 Feb 2009). The final function has "10 poles and six zeroes": numerator `1.06 s^3 (s+109.9)(s+34.0)(s+7.41)`, denominator `(ladder polynomial)(s+97.5)(s+38.5)(s+4.45)(s+578.1)(s+20.0)(s+7.41) + 18.7 k s^4 (s+46.5)(s+4.40)` with `k` the resonance 0 to 1 and `s` in rad/s (same page, update 19 Jul 2009; the file `?pge=diode2` repeats it and shows five groups of coupling capacitors in a diagram I could not see as text). Tag: SCHEMATIC + SPICE ("based on SPICE simulation, so take it for what its worth"). The pole at 578.1 rad/s is 92 Hz; 97.5 is 15.5 Hz; 38.5 is 6.1 Hz; 20.0 is 3.2 Hz; 7.41 is 1.2 Hz; 4.45 is 0.7 Hz.
- Measured: "The lower resonant peak is very real: ... I took some measurements from my TBX-303 clone, and the test set-up ... was adding just enough capacitive load to enable the filter to oscillate comfortably at around 8Hz" (`...?pge=diode2`, last paragraph). MEASURED on a clone whose analog section follows the 303's, with a loaded test set-up; do not read it as a property of the stock unit.
- Also: a1k0n's x0xb0x measurement puts the main feedback highpass "near 100 Hz" (first transcript, from `303.js:34`); Open303 uses 150 Hz. The 92 Hz pole above supports a1k0n's number (SCHEMATIC, two sources) over Open303's 150.
- And: "In the TB-303, 32 Hz was down by 5 dB" (Devil Fish manual, "New Functionality", SECONDARY: Whittle comparing his machine to the stock one).
- Spec 1 said: feedback highpass 150 Hz (Open303), `highpass1` 44.486 Hz, allpass 14.008 Hz, `highpass2` 24.167 Hz, notch 7.5164 Hz bandwidth 4.7 (Open303's fitted values).
- Change: the feedback highpass cutoff to 92 Hz instead of 150 Hz (internal constant `feedbackHighpass`), or to about 100 Hz to match a1k0n; this raises the resonant peak at low cutoffs slightly and brings it closer to the SPICE model. Keep Open303's output highpass chain unless the bench shows the bass is far from "32 Hz down 5 dB". Confidence medium: the two analyses agree on roughly 90 to 100 Hz and Open303 chose 150 by ear.

### 1.4 Cutoff range

- States: "The maximum resonant frequency of the filter, with no Env Mod or Filter Tracking, is approximately 5 kHz. This is an octave above the typical maximum for the TB-303" (Devil Fish manual, "New Functionality"). So the stock top end is about 2.5 kHz (SECONDARY, a builder's figure for a typical unit, with no method given). "Filter Cut Off pot range doubled to 5 kHz max, and widened to include much lower frequencies" (manual feature list). "Filter Cut Off: Between about 10.30 and 3 o'clock" is the stated limit to reproduce a stock unit (manual, "Limiting the Devil Fish to TB-303 sounds").
- Minimum: not stated in any source I read. Open303's author measured 314 Hz to 2394 Hz at Env Mod 0 (first transcript, from `rosic_Open303.cpp:297-298`); a1k0n's fit puts the floor at about 288 Hz at the lowest setting (`303.js:158-160`, MEASURED on an x0xb0x, via the R69 voltage).
- Spec 1 said: cutoff 314 to 2394 Hz nominal. Agreed (2394 against "about 2.5 kHz" is within the builder's accuracy). No change.
- Pot taper: the resonance pot is a dual 50 k linear ("B") and Tuning and Accent are 50 k linear (Devil Fish manual, parts list near the end and the 303-mods page: "Tuning: 50K B (Linear). Resonance: Dual 50K B ... Accent: 50K B (Linear)"). The cutoff knob's taper is not stated in what I read; Open303 maps it exponentially from measurements. The UI should show Resonance, Accent and Tuning as linear knobs.

### 1.5 Main envelope (MEG): decay range and what accent does to it

- States: "On non-accented notes, the TB-303's Main Envelope Generator (MEG) had a decay time between 200 ms and 2 seconds – as controlled by the Decay pot. On accented notes, the decay time was fixed to 200 ms." (Devil Fish manual, "New and Changed Controls"). Whittle, 1999: "On accented notes, this is shorted out and MEG runs to the relatively short time which is the same as when the Decay pot is fully anti-clockwise" (`303-unique.html`, MEG section). SECONDARY (his reading of the circuit; two statements agree on 200 ms).
- Also: "the circuitry does not allow [Env Mod] to be turned down to zero even when the pot is fully anti-clockwise. (The Devil Fish allows this and extends the amount of Env Mod when the pot is fully clockwise.)" (`303-unique.html`) and the manual: "the upper limit in the Devil Fish is about 3 times what it was in the TB-303". SECONDARY.
- A note on the definition: the sources do not say whether "200 ms to 2 s" is a time constant or a time to a given level. I read it as a time constant (Open303 uses tau = 200 to 2000 ms and its author measured the filter against real units).
- Spec 1 said: decay knob 200 to 2000 ms tau; accent notes 200 ms tau fixed, ignoring the knob. Agreed by both statements. No change. (The build already does this, line 134 and line 209.)
- Whittle's description of the MEG: "Sharp attack, exponential decay" and it drives the filter through Env Mod; on accented notes it also goes through "a switch (which is only on during accented notes)" and then the Accent pot into the VCA and the accent sweep circuit (see 1.6, 1.8).

### 1.6 The accent circuit: how the sweep forms, component values, stacking, the role of resonance

- States (the key passage, `303-unique.html`, "The real fun is in the 'Accent Sweep Circuit'"): "a diode and a 47k resistor in series driving the ACW (anti-clockwise) end of a 100k pot, and a 1uF capacitor (to ground) hanging off the CW end. The wiper of the pot goes via a 100k mixing resistor to one of the two summing nodes for filter frequency." "This pot is the second section of the Resonance pot." Pot ACW: "the filter is driven largely with the output of the MEG ... basically 100/147 of the MEG voltage minus the capacitor voltage". Pot CW: "the pulse of voltage driving the filter is smoothed by coming from the 1uF cap as it is charged via the diode and 147k of resistance". Stacking: "Since the capacitor has not discharged fully from the one before, the second and subsequent response curves go *higher*". The Devil Fish manual names the capacitor C13 and adds: "when the resonance pot is fully clockwise, this sweeps upwards and some charge remains in a capacitor (C13) by the time the next accent occurs. Consequently the second and subsequent accent pulses cause a higher output than the first". Tag: SCHEMATIC (component values and topology as read by Whittle from the circuit of a real unit); his curves are drawn by hand in ASCII, not measured.
- The VCA path from the same source: "It adds to the control current of the VCA. (Via an RC network of a 47k and a 0.033uF to soften the attack a little.) This is the primary reason why accented notes are louder." The Accent pot is in series with the MEG output before both paths.
- What the knobs do: the Accent knob scales the MEG voltage entering both paths. The Resonance knob's second gang sets where the wiper sits between the diode end (direct, short pulse) and the capacitor end (smoothed, delayed, stacking). The Devil Fish adds a "Slow" mode "with a much longer time-constant. The output takes longer to rise, but it can rise twice as high" and a "Fast" mode in which "subsequent accents produce a smaller output" (manual, "New and Changed Controls"): in stock "Normal" it is the circuit above.
- Time constants (MY-CALC from the stated values; ideal diode, no drop): charging at the CW end goes through 47 k + 100 k = 147 k into 1 uF: tau about 147 ms. The cap discharges through the 100 k mixing resistor: tau about 100 ms. At the ACW end the wiper sees the 47 k / 100 k divider (100/147 of the MEG), so the shape follows the MEG (tau 200 ms on accent). The VCA RC is 47 k x 0.033 uF = 1.55 ms.
- MY-CALC of stacking (a circuit simulation I wrote: ideal diode and switch, MEG jumps to 1 at each accented step and decays with tau 200 ms, the accent pot treated as a plain gain of 1, sixteenth notes at 130 BPM = 115 ms; the output is the wiper voltage, proportional to the current added to the filter): at the CW end the first accent peaks at 0.242 of the MEG peak, about 103 ms after the note start; the second peaks at 0.305 (57 ms after), the third 0.319, the fourth 0.321: about +33 % over the first, saturating by the third. At the ACW end the first accent peaks at 0.515 immediately; the second 0.585, the third 0.615, the fourth 0.627 (+22 %), because the capacitor still shares the pot. At mid wiper: 0.287 at 57 ms, then 0.380, 0.419, 0.431. A plain gap of four steps lets the CW-end sweep fall back to the first-accent shape (0.243 against 0.242). These are shapes from the quoted component values, not measurements; the diode's forward drop, the Accent pot's own resistance and the MEG's true peak voltage are not in the sources, and change the absolute heights.
- How the sweep enters the filter: the filter frequency is set by a control current; the Env Mod and accent currents sum at "two summing nodes" (Whittle). a1k0n measured "the filter cutoff is directly proportional to the current" (README) and fitted the cutoff as linear in the R69 current, `27.6 Hz/mV + 103 Hz` (`303.js:157-160`, MEASURED on an x0xb0x). So the accent sweep adds Hz (linearly with the control current), it is not an exponent added in octaves.
- Spec 1 said: the accent adds `accentGain * rc2(MEG)` octaves with rc2 a 15 ms smoother (Open303), "no inter-note memory and no resonance dependence". Build: the sweep RC has tau 15 ms at resonance 0 rising to 100 ms ("prior") at resonance 1, added as octaves (line 137, line 228-232).
- Change for the build (processor internal; no new parameter): replace the RC on the exponent by the circuit above as a small numerical model per sample (or per block): state `Vc` for the 1 uF cap; the resonance knob sets the wiper position `w = resonance` (linear pot, 0 = ACW end); an ideal diode from the accent-gated MEG through 47 k to the ACW end; 100 k pot split at `w`; 100 k to the summing node; cap at the CW end. Add the wiper current to the filter control as a term in Hz: `fc = fBase(cutoff, envMod, MEG) + accentGain * accentHz * Vw` where `accentHz` is a calibration constant (the sources give no Hz figure for the accent term; set it so that a full-accent, resonance-0 note's peak is about +1 octave above the unaccented sweep, which is the Open303 figure, then let A9 compare). Also add the 0.45 x MEG and the accent term to the VCA through the 47 k / 0.033 uF RC (1.55 ms low-pass) instead of Open303's 4.0 x MEG with a 200 Hz biquad de-clicker. Confidence: topology and component values high (one detailed source and a second, the manual, naming C13); the two Hz and volt calibrations low.

### 1.7 Slide: time constant, which step carries the flag, constant time

- States (circuit): "The usual rather fast slide of a TB-303 is caused by the 100K resistance of the DAC feeding a 0.22uF capacitor." (Devil Fish manual, "Slide Slew Times"); "Normally the slide time is 60 ms (milliseconds)" ("New and Changed Controls"); and "The times mentioned above ... refer to the approximate audible time it takes to reach the final frequency. But in any resistor capacitor arrangement, the final voltage is never precisely reached". Tag: SCHEMATIC (the R and C values) and SECONDARY (the 60 ms figure, an approximate audible time).
- MY-CALC: tau = R x C = 100 k x 0.22 uF = 22 ms. 60 ms is 2.7 tau, i.e. 93 % of the way; 99 % takes about 100 ms.
- What it slews: "how long it takes for the internal CV to the Oscillator (and Filter Tracking Pot and CV Out) to slew from one level (and therefore oscillator pitch) to another" (manual): the slide capacitor sits on the pitch control voltage, which is volts per octave; so the glide is an RC in the pitch (octave) domain, not in Hz (the exponential converter follows the CV). The slide acts on the oscillator, not on the filter (the manual lists "Filter Tracking" as a separate, optional thing and says stock does not track).
- Which step: Whittle (`303-slide.html`): "If there is a slide programmed on that note, then the slide circuit is actually activated on the *next* note. The slide starts at the start of clock-pulse 0 and ends at the end of clock-pulse 5. The other function of slide is that the note it is programmed on does not turn off, but the gate stays high and runs into the next." His algorithm: "1 - The slide signal will go high 1/16 note after the start of slid note ... 2 - The slide signal will remain active until the end of the note which follows the slide note." He cautions on the page (2012) that "Perhaps some of what I wrote below is wrong". Tag: MEASURED on his unit with a scope (he says he investigated "how the TB-303's sequencer worked"), with the 2012 caveat.
- Constant time or constant rate: constant tau (an RC), so the audible duration is nearly the same for a small and a large interval, and the velocity is highest at the start (in octaves per second). Not a constant-rate glide.
- Spec 1 said: tau = 0.2 x slideTime, so 12 ms at the 60 ms default, exponential in Hz (Open303); `ev.slide` on a note means gate held into the next and glide into it; a note flagged with slide does not retrigger the envelope.
- Change for the build: (a) default `slideTime` stays 60 (the audible time) but the glide must be tau = `slideTime` / 2.7 (22 ms at the default), not 0.2 x `slideTime`; (b) apply the RC to the pitch in octaves (semitones), not to frequency: `pitchNow += (pitchTarget - pitchNow) * (1 - exp(-dt / tau))` then `f = 440 * 2^((pitchNow - 69) / 12)`; (c) the glide runs through the note that follows the flagged one (state active from the start of the next note to its end) and the accent state applied is the next note's; the build already does not retrigger on a slid note (line 217). Confidence: tau 22 ms high (two values stated by the unit's builder); the audible-60 ms equivalence medium; pitch-domain versus Hz-domain high (the glide is on the CV).

### 1.8 Gate length, ties, and the VCA envelope

- States (clock): "Lets say you have 16 1/16th notes programmed. Each note occupies six clock pulses ... The gate goes high at the start of clock pulse 0 - the positive edge. The gate goes low half-way through clock pulse 3 - on its negative edge. That is for a normal, 1/16 note. So there are 3.5 clock pulses on and 2.5 off." (`303-slide.html`). So the gate is 3.5/6 = 58.3 % of a step on a normal note (MEASURED on his unit, 1999, caveat as in 1.7). The sequencer has 24 clock pulses per quarter note (6 per sixteenth), MY-CALC from "six clock pulses" per 1/16.
- States (VCA): "Volume Envelope Generator (VEG): Sharp attack, exponential decay, fixed decay and rather long time ... Drives the VCA and does nothing else." (`303-unique.html`); "The TB-303's VEG decay was fixed at ~ 3 to 4 seconds"; "In the TB-303 there was a (typical) 4 ms delay and then a 3 ms attack time"; "In the TB-303, the start of the audible note was between 1 and 5 ms (typically 4 ms) after the start of the gate signal"; "In the TB-303, the decay at the end of an unaccented note was about 16 ms – 8 ms of normal volume and 8 ms of linear decay" (Devil Fish manual, "New and Changed Controls" and "New Functionality"). SECONDARY for all (his description of stock behaviour as it differs from the Devil Fish; the delay he notes as a defect of the stock unit). Accented notes: the VCA also receives the MEG (1.6), "Note that in a TB-303, the MEG time is fixed and short for accented notes."
- Ties: the slide flag holds the gate across the boundary (1.7). A plain tie in time mode is also described on the card as a note-length choice (1.10); the sources I read give the gate behaviour only for the slide case. I did not find a statement that a tie differs from a slide in the gate timing; assume the gate is held through the tied step.
- Spec 1 said: amp envelope attack 0, decay tau 1230 ms, release 1 ms normal and 50 ms accent (Open303), gate = the step length in Open303's own sequencer, with the engine's `dur` in this project. Build: ampDecay 1.23 s, normal release in the constants block, accent release 0.05 s.
- Change: (a) the gate belongs to whoever sets `dur`: the sequencer should send `dur` = 0.583 x the step length for a normal note (and let it run to the next step's start when `slide` is set); (b) in the VCA: delay 4 ms from gate to the audible start, then a 3 ms linear-ish attack (replace "attack 0"), release: hold full level 8 ms after gate-off, then a linear ramp to zero over 8 ms (replace the exponential 1 ms / 50 ms release; no accent-specific release is stated in the sources: drop the 50 ms accent release or keep it as a prior); (c) the long VEG decay stays; the manual's "3 to 4 seconds" and Open303's tau 1.23 s are not in conflict if 3 to 4 s is a time to a low level (tau 1.23 s reaches 8 % at 3 s), but this is my reading. Confidence: gate 58.3 % medium-high (one measurement, flagged by its author), delay/attack/release medium (builder's statement).

### 1.10 Square wave: shape, duty, and level against the saw

- States: nothing in the pages I could read gives the square's shape or duty or the saw/square level. Two search summaries (not pages I read: KVR and the x0xb0x wiki, hosts refused, see section 0): "A user in an Open303 forum thread reports that the duty cycle varies between units, with one 303 measured near 86-87% and another near 98%" and "the saw is sent to Q8, where it is shaped into a square" with "2SA733ap parts that are no longer produced". These are SEEN-SNIPPET only (the search tool's own summary of those pages) and I cannot say what "86-87 %" and "98 %" refer to (high fraction? at which note?). The same summary says "Another Open303 developer measured duty cycle against note number on a real 303", so the duty may depend on pitch.

## 2. The sequencer as the hardware has it

Read from the procedure card images (black and white, from `.../tb-303-card/`) unless marked MEM; the Devil Fish manual repeats some of it.

- Mode selector (a four-position rotary): TRACK WRITE, TRACK PLAY, PATTERN PLAY, PATTERN WRITE (the card's front-panel diagram, label B). A second rotary, "TRACK / PATT. GROUP" (label C), picks the track (1 to 7: Wikipedia says 7 songs, SECONDARY) or the pattern group I to IV.
- Patterns: pattern group (I to IV) x selector keys 1 to 8 x section A or B = 64 patterns (arithmetic; Wikipedia: "64 patterns"). Step number: "While holding the FUNCTION button down, press the SELECTOR switch marked STEP once for each step" (card, "Setting the step number"): the pattern length is set by repeated presses of the STEP key, up to 16 (Whittle: "16 1/16th notes", and the first transcript's Wikipedia infobox "16 pattern keys"; no statement of the maximum on the card pages I looked at, so 16 is READ-by-inference, not quoted).
- Writing a pattern (card "Writing bass patterns"): set the mode to PATTERN WRITE; choose the memory position with the selector keys, group and section; hold the selected switch and press PATTERN CLEAR to clear; press PITCH MODE, then play the keys (the thirteen note keys C to C) in order; press FUNCTION when done. Accent and slide are set in pitch mode: "While listening to each sound using the TAP button, press the SELECTOR switch marked ACCENT for an accent, and the SELECTOR switch marked SLIDE for a slide" (card steps 13 to 15). So the note, accent and slide are three data per step. TAP is the WRITE/NEXT button (big button, far right).
- Time mode ("Writing the length of the notes", card steps 11 and 12): press TIME MODE, then use three SELECTOR keys marked with note-length symbols to write the length of the note. The key legends above DOWN, UP and ACCENT show three symbols (an eighth-note glyph with a dot, a second eighth-note glyph, and a dash with a rest-like glyph); I interpret the three states as note, tie and rest, matching my memory of the machine (MEM): the card text does not name them.
- Rests: written in time mode (above); in TRACK WRITE the "BACK" key (label Q) carries a rest glyph: "Setting the [rest glyph]: ... Press the [rest glyph] button" writes a rest measure into a track (card, "Setting the rest", steps 1 to 4).
- Transpose: the DOWN and UP keys (octave down and up), and "In pattern play mode, you can press 'Pitch' and one of the 12 keys to transpose the pattern up by 0 to 12 semitones" (Devil Fish manual, "CV Out"; the card: "When shifting, hold the PITCH MODE button down, and press the key-switch"). Pitch CV: "The internal sequencer produces a voltage in the range of 1 to 5 volts. This represents the 3 octave range of a pattern which can be transposed by up to an octave. 2 Volts is the normal C at the left of the keyboard, 1 volt is the C an octave below. 3 Volts is the C at the right of the 'keyboard' and 4 volts is that C transposed up an octave." (manual).
- Edit keys: with FUNCTION held, the keys marked DEL (on C#) and INS (on D#) delete or insert a step or measure (card, "Deletion" and "Insertion"). The BAR button (the FUNCTION key's second legend) and the selector digits (1 to 9, 0, 100, 200 along the bottom of the key area) designate a measure number in track mode. BAR RESET and D.C. (da capo) buttons at the far left, RUN/STOP below them. The tempo knob (SLOW to FAST) at the far left of the second row.
- Playing: RUN/STOP; in TRACK PLAY a track is a chain of patterns; PATTERN PLAY plays one pattern; "Playing a bass line (alternative method)": set the pattern group and selector, press RUN/STOP.
- Clock: 24 pulses per quarter note (MY-CALC from Whittle's six per sixteenth). The sequencer plays "in perfect time with the clock pulses" (the page says this of the 808; for the 303 the same clock-pulse timing is described, with no swing).

## 3. The panel, in words to draw from

Basis: the card's front-panel diagram (a black and white halftone, 670 x 392 px, which I enlarged and looked at) for the layout and the labels (READ); colours, materials and sizes are not in anything I read and are marked MEM. No maker's logo or wordmark will be drawn; the HVT-303 carries its own wordmark.

Three horizontal bands inside one silver case; the lower two bands share a slightly recessed black bar behind the keys.

Band 1, the top strip (a narrow light band), left to right:
1. At far left a maker's logo (not to be drawn) and, above it, two small legend groups with a triangle mark: "MIX IN" (a jack) and "WAVEFORM" with two tiny waveform icons (a saw and a square) over a small two-position switch; then "SYNC IN" (a jack).
2. A thin vertical separator line.
3. Six equally spaced round knobs, each with a ring of tick marks around its top three quarters, with the legend above in small capitals, left to right: TUNING, CUT OFF FREQ, RESONANCE, ENV MOD, DECAY, ACCENT. (On the card the TUNING and ACCENT knobs and the row marked F are called out.) Each cap is a dark ring around a lighter face with a raised pointer; the first knob (TUNING) is not separated from the rest except by its label.
4. A second vertical separator, then five small jack markers with triangles: CV, GATE, HEADPHONE, OUTPUT, DC IN.
5. At far right, a script wordmark (the maker's "Bass Line" legend; not to be drawn).

Band 2, the control band (slightly darker strip):
1. TEMPO: a large round knob at far left, SLOW at its lower left and FAST at its lower right.
2. TRACK / PATT. GROUP: a rotary selector with a rectangular slotted cap, with legends around it: "TRACK" in a small black box, "PATT. GROUP", positions marked 1, 2 (at the left), 3, 4 (top, roman numerals I, II, III, IV on the diagram), 5, 6, 7 (right).
3. MODE: a rotary selector of the same style, with four positions on its right: "WRITE" and "PLAY" (grouped under "TRACK") and "PLAY" and "WRITE" (grouped under "PATTERN").
4. The model legend "TB-303 / Computer Controlled" in black (not to be drawn; use HVT-303).
5. VOLUME: a large round knob at far right, with the power switch built into it: "POWER SW OFF" at its left end and "MAX" at its right.

Band 3, the keys (a black bar behind them):
1. Far left, from top to bottom: a small button pair "D.C." and "BAR RESET" with a small PATTERN CLEAR button beneath; below it a large RUN/STOP button with a small LED (marked "BATTERY") above it.
2. Next, a tall FUNCTION button with a "BAR" legend under it and an LED above it; beside it, two small legends "NORMAL MODE" and "PATTERN" with an LED.
3. A wide black key-shaped legend "PITCH MODE" over the first key, with a button for it at the left.
4. The key row: 13 rocker-style keys drawn as a piano octave. Eight white keys (C D E F G A B C) in a row with their number legends 1 to 8 on a black strip below them ("SELECTOR"), and five black keys (C# D# F# G# A#) above, in the usual 2-and-3 grouping. Each black key has a small LED above it and a legend: the C# key carries "DEL", the D# key "INS". Each white key has an LED above its button and the number below.
5. Right of the keys, four more keys with LEDs: TRANSPOSE DOWN, TRANSPOSE UP (a pair joined by a label "TRANSPOSE"), then ACCENT and SLIDE; their selector numbers are 9 (with the legend STEP), 0, 100 and 200; the last two also carry "PATT SECTION" with the letters A and B.
6. Above those four keys, the TIME MODE legend with its LED, over three symbols (see 2).
7. At far right: a "BACK" button (with a rest glyph) above a large "WRITE/NEXT" button that is also marked TAP (a "D.S." legend sits above it).

Colours and materials (MEM, not read; the card is black and white): the top of the case is silver, brushed aluminium-like; the legends are black; the knob caps are dark with a light silver centre; the keys are the grey-white and black of a miniature keyboard; the LEDs are red. I cannot give hex values. The case is about 300 mm wide and 160 mm deep (MEM). The panel layout above is not to scale: on the diagram the top strip is about 60 px high, the control band about 80 px and the key band about 140 px of the 392 px figure; the key row is about 60 % of the width.

Which controls are in which band (for P4): the waveform switch is in band 1 left of the six knobs (not among them); Volume, Tempo and the two rotaries are in band 2; Tuning belongs in the knob row, not with Volume. The six pots Tuning to Accent are the "six small pots" Whittle names; he counts "five pots (not counting the tuning pot and the square/sawtooth switch)" as the sound-defining set (Devil Fish manual).

## 4. The five corrections that change the sound most, in order

1. Accent sweep: replace Open303's 15 ms smoother on the cutoff exponent (and the build's 15 to 100 ms prior) with the real network: diode and 47 k into a 100 k pot (the Resonance knob's second gang), a 1 uF capacitor at the CW end, 100 k to the filter summing node; the output adds Hz, and consecutive accents stack (about +33 % by the third at resonance 1, 16ths at 130 BPM, MY-CALC), the first sweep peak arriving about 100 ms into the note at high resonance. Source: Whittle `303-unique.html` and the Devil Fish manual (C13). SCHEMATIC. Confidence: structure high, absolute heights low.
2. Resonance: cap the feedback below the ideal critical gain (stock maximum between 8.5 and 17 in core-gain units, from "double the usual feedback" and Routh-Hurwitz on Stinchcombe's `H_tb`), keep the feedback highpass near 92 to 100 Hz (not 150), and let the closed-loop DC gain fall as 1/(1+G). Source: Stinchcombe `?pge=diode`, `?pge=diode2`; Devil Fish manual. SCHEMATIC + SECONDARY. Confidence: bracket high, point inside it low.
3. Slide: tau 22 ms (100 k x 0.22 uF), applied to the pitch in octaves, active through the note after the flagged one with the gate held. Source: Devil Fish manual "Slide Slew Times"; Whittle `303-slide.html`. SCHEMATIC + MEASURED (slide timing, with his 2012 caveat). Confidence: high.
4. VCA envelope and gate: 4 ms delay and 3 ms attack at the note start, an 8 ms hold and 8 ms linear release at the end, gate = 3.5/6 of a sixteenth (58.3 %). Source: Devil Fish manual "New and Changed Controls", "New Functionality"; Whittle `303-slide.html`. SECONDARY + MEASURED. Confidence: medium.
5. Accented-note VCA path: on accented notes the MEG, scaled by the Accent pot, adds to the VCA control current through 47 k + 0.033 uF (1.55 ms); Open303 instead uses 0.45 x MEG on every note plus 4.0 x accent x MEG and a 200 Hz de-click filter. Use the circuit's structure (accent-gated MEG x Accent pot, 1.55 ms smoothing) and keep Open303's 0.45 and 4.0 only as starting calibration. Source: Whittle `303-unique.html`. SCHEMATIC. Confidence: structure high; the gain relative to the unaccented note low (calibrate by ear and A9).
