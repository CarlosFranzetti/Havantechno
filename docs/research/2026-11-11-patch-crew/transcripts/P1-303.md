## 2. The signal path

### 2.1 Oscillator (SEEN-CODE)

One VCO (an analog sawtooth core). Open303 builds both waves as mip-mapped wavetables (`rosic_MipMappedWaveTable.cpp`):

- `fillWithSaw303` (lines 270-289): a ramp that rises 0 to +1 over the first half of the table and goes from -1 to 0 over the second half (a sawtooth with its jump in the middle, i.e. a plain saw with a 50 % phase shift). In `Open303::getSample` the oscillator output is negated (`tmp = -oscillator.getSample();`, `rosic_Open303.h:387`), so the audible saw falls through the cycle and jumps up, like the hardware's.
- `fillWithSquare303` (lines 245-268): the same ramp, then `tanh(tanhShaperFactor * x + tanhShaperOffset)` with polarity switched, then a circular shift by `squarePhaseShift` degrees. Constants (`rosic_MipMappedWaveTable.cpp:12-14`): `tanhShaperFactor = dB2amp(36.9)` (a gain of 69.8), `tanhShaperOffset = 4.37`, `squarePhaseShift = 180.0`. Reading the numbers: the tanh saturates at +1 wherever 69.8 x + 4.37 is large, so the square is high while the ramp value x is above -0.0626 and low below it; that is a pulse of roughly 53 % high and 47 % low (derived, MY-CALC; the ramp x runs -1 to 0 over half the cycle, so the low part is 0.937 of that half). One edge is the saw's hard jump; the other is the ramp crossing, softened by the tanh over about 1.4 % of the half cycle. This matches the folklore that the 303 square is a saw squashed by a transistor stage, not a 50 % square (MEM, UNVERIFIED for the hardware).
- Both tables are band-limited by the mip-map; the oscillator runs at 4x oversampling (`static const int oversampling = 4`, `rosic_Open303.h:280`).
- Pitch: `pitchToFreq(note, tuning)` with A4 = `tuning` Hz (default 440); the sequencer notes sit at the engine's own octave offset (`key + 12*octave + currentNote`).

### 2.2 The diode-ladder lowpass (SEEN-CODE, SEEN-SNIPPET, SEEN-CODE a1k0n)

What differs from the Moog transistor ladder:

- Four coupled one-pole stages (diode ladder, each stage's state also feeds its neighbours), not four independent cascaded stages. Open303's `TB_303` branch of `TeeBeeFilter::getSample` (`rosic_TeeBeeFilter.h:284-296`):
  ```
  y0  = in - feedbackHighpass(k*y4);
  y1 += 2*b0*(y0 - y1 + y2);
  y2 +=   b0*(y1 - 2*y2 + y3);
  y3 +=   b0*(y2 - 2*y3 + y4);
  y4 +=   b0*(y3 - 2*y4);
  return 2*g*y4;
  ```
  The first stage has twice the coupling coefficient (2*b0). This is the signature of the 303 ladder: a1k0n's `filt303.py` says the model is "a moog 4-pole diode ladder model w/ one of the capacitors halved (why did they do this? not sure)", with the denominator `(1+s/wf)*(1+s/wc)^3*(1+2s/wc) + fb*(s/wf)` where `wf` is the feedback highpass and `fb` the feedback strength.
- Pole spacing and the "18 dB" impression: the four poles are not coincident like the idealised Moog cascade; the coupling spreads them (a1k0n's pole tables show one real pole and two complex pairs at each setting). The nominal slope is 24 dB/oct (Wikipedia infobox; a1k0n `303.js:403` comment "four poles total, 24dB/octave rolloff"), but the first stage and the high-pass behaviour below make the audible slope read shallower than 24 dB on a spectrum plot (MY-PORT: at 1 kHz cutoff, resonance 0, the response is -18.9 dB at the cutoff, -32.8 dB at 2x, -51.5 dB at 4x, -73.6 dB at 8x: roughly 14 dB then 19 dB then 22 dB per octave approaching 24). So "18 dB/oct" in forum lore is the effective slope near and just above the cutoff, not a different pole count. The wave table says the same: the filter is 4 poles.
- Resonance feedback goes through a one-pole highpass: Open303 `filter.setFeedbackHighpassCutoff(150.0)` (`rosic_Open303.cpp:73`, also `rosic_TeeBeeFilter.cpp:19`). a1k0n measured the same effect on an x0xb0x (`README.md`: "works almost exactly like the theoretical model says it should, except for a high-pass filter in the resonance feedback loop") and put it near 100 Hz ("`303.js:34`: The first pole/zero pair forms a 100Hz high-pass filter ... formed by C27/R113 + C25/R114"). Effect: the resonance is thin at low cutoffs and the bass is not boosted ("without it, it's too resonant at lower frequencies and sounds like it's whistling fifths and octaves", `303.js:36-38`).
- Other highpass elements between the filter and the output (SEEN-CODE and a1k0n): a pre-filter highpass `highpass1` at 44.486 Hz in front of the ladder, and after it an allpass at 14.008 Hz, `highpass2` at 24.167 Hz and a notch at 7.5164 Hz with bandwidth 4.7 (`rosic_Open303.cpp:65-71`). a1k0n: "The 303 actually loses quite a lot of low-end bass sound between the filter and amplifier" (`README.md`).
- Resonance does not self-oscillate (Wikipedia infobox "non-self-oscillating"). Open303 gets this by mapping resonance to feedback k so that at full resonance k is about 17 at low cutoff (`rosic_TeeBeeFilter.h:260`, the constant term 16.998792; the diode ladder's critical gain is 17, versus 4 for the Moog ladder) and with the first-stage and highpass details the loop is at the edge of oscillation, ringing but not sustaining. MY-PORT: at 1 kHz cutoff, resonance 100 %, the peak is +5.1 dB at about 1025 Hz (relative to a unit sine in; the passband gain at resonance 0 is +2 dB because of the factor 2 g); at 3 kHz cutoff the peak is +14.1 dB; at resonance 50 % the peak is about -0.2 dB at 1 kHz. Peak height grows with cutoff because the 150 Hz feedback highpass bites less (MY-PORT).
- Resonance skew (`rosic_TeeBeeFilter.h:166-172`): `r = (1 - exp(-3 x)) / (1 - exp(-3))`, x = resonance in 0..1. r = 0, 0.555, 0.818, 0.941, 1.0 at x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1 (MY-PORT).
- Cutoff-dependent coefficients in the `TB_303` branch (`rosic_TeeBeeFilter.h:256-265`, tuned "ala mystran & kunn (page 40 in the kvr-thread)", line 48), with `wc = 2 pi cutoff / fs`, `fx = wc * 0.70710678 / (2 pi)`, fs = 4 x 44100 in Open303:
  - `b0 = (0.00045522346 + 6.1922189 fx) / (1 + 12.358354 fx + 4.4156345 fx^2)`
  - `k = fx (fx (fx (fx (fx (fx + 7198.6997) - 5837.7917) - 476.47308) + 614.95611) + 213.87126) + 16.998792`
  - `g = k/17; g = (g - 1) r + 1; g = g (1 + r); k = k r`
  - cutoff clamp: 200 Hz to 20000 Hz (`rosic_TeeBeeFilter.h:154-159`, "an absolute floor for the cutoff frequency - tweakable").
  - The code ships a second branch for other modes (a 12th-order polynomial `pa00..pa12` for the one-pole coefficient and an 8th-order `pr0..pr8` for the feedback scale, lines 217-252); the 303 mode overrides it.

### 2.3 VCA

A multiplier driven by the amplitude envelope (section 3). Open303: `tmp *= ampEnvOut; tmp *= ampScaler;` (`rosic_Open303.h:400`). There is also a de-click lowpass on the envelope: `ampDeClicker`, BiquadFilter LOWPASS12 at 200 Hz (`rosic_Open303.cpp:51-53`). The gain term `amp2dB(sqrt(0.5))` in line 52 looks like a bug in the source (a linear value passed as dB); P5 should not copy it.

## 3. Envelopes, accent, slide (SEEN-CODE)

### 3.1 Filter envelope (Open303 "mainEnv")

- A pure exponential decay, 1 to 0, with time constant tau in ms (`rosic_DecayEnvelope.h`, `getSample(){ y *= c; return y; }`). Triggered only by `triggerNote`, not by `slideToNote` (`rosic_Open303.cpp:213-265`: `mainEnv.trigger()` appears only in `triggerNote`). So a slide does not restart the filter sweep or the amp envelope.
- Decay knob: normal decay tau = 200 ms to 2000 ms, exponential knob taper (`Open303VST.cpp:236`: `setDecay(linToExp(value, 0, 1, 200.0, 2000.0))`). Class default `normalDecay = 1000.0` (`rosic_Open303.cpp:21`).
- Cutoff modulation: `instCutoff = cutoff * 2^(tmp1 + tmp2)`, `tmp1 = envScaler * (mainEnv - envOffset)`, `tmp2 = accentGain * rc2(mainEnv)` (`rosic_Open303.h:366-372`). So the cutoff sweeps exponentially (in octaves) from the peak at note start down to a floor below the nominal knob cutoff.
- Env Mod and cutoff scaling, "useMeasured mapping = true" (`rosic_Open303.cpp:291-313`). Let `e = envMod/100`, `c = log(cutoff/c0)/log(c1/c0)`. Constants: `c0 = 313.8152786059267` Hz (lowest nominal cutoff), `c1 = 2394.411986817546` Hz (highest), `oF = 0.048292930943553`, `oC = 0.294391201442418`, `sLoF = 3.773996325111173`, `sLoC = 0.736965594166206`, `sHiF = 4.194548788411135`, `sHiC = 0.864344900642434`. Then `sLo = sLoF e + sLoC`, `sHi = sHiF e + sHiC`, `envScaler = (1-c) sLo + c sHi`, `envOffset = oF c + oC`. These are the author's fits to measurements of a real unit (the VST calls `setCutoff(3.138e2)` / `setCutoff(2.394e3)` at the two ends with env mod 0 and 100, `Open303VST.cpp:70-74`).
- What this means (MY-PORT, table below): Env Mod does not just add a sweep on top of the knob cutoff; it also lowers the floor. With the knob at 1000 Hz, Env Mod 0 gives a sweep from 1463 Hz down to 835 Hz; Env Mod 50 gives 3758 Hz down to 533 Hz; Env Mod 100 gives 9650 Hz down to 341 Hz.

  | Cutoff knob (Hz) | Env Mod | Peak (Hz, t=0) | Floor (Hz, decayed) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 314 | 0 | 450 | 270 |
  | 314 | 100 | 2852 | 125 (clamped to 200 by the filter) |
  | 1000 | 0 | 1463 | 835 |
  | 1000 | 50 | 3758 | 533 |
  | 1000 | 100 | 9650 | 341 |
  | 2394 | 0 | 3549 | 1950 |
  | 2394 | 50 | 9229 | 1185 |
  | 2394 | 100 | 23996 (clamped to 20000) | 720 |

- Cross-check from a1k0n (measured on an x0xb0x, SEEN-CODE `303.js:157-170`, knob values 0..1, no licence so facts only): `e0 = exp(5.22617147 + 1.70418937*cutoff - 0.68382928*envmod) + 103` Hz (floor), `e1 = exp(5.55921003 + 2.17788267*cutoff + 1.99224351*envmod) + 103` Hz (peak). "These are from measuring the voltage across R69 on my x0xb0x while holding the gate high or low, and linearly fitting the cutoff frequency to current (appears to be about 27.6Hz/mV_R69 + 103 Hz)". With cutoff 0.5, envmod 0.5 this gives floor about 435 Hz and peak about 1685 Hz; the two fits disagree on the sweep depth by a factor of two or more at the middle (they use different cutoff knob definitions). A9 cannot settle that without a reference recording; use Open303's numbers (MIT, ours to port) and keep a1k0n's as a sanity band.
- a1k0n's decay: `d = (0.1 + decay) * 44100` samples to fall to 1/10, so 0.1 s to 1.1 s for a knob 0..1 (tau 43 to 478 ms). That is much shorter than Open303's tau of 200 to 2000 ms (to 1/10 would be 0.46 s to 4.6 s). Different definitions (a1k0n's is the decay of the current, Open303's is the decay of an internal exponent), so not a contradiction, but the audible result differs: expect Open303's envelope to feel long at the top of the knob.

### 3.2 Amplitude envelope

- `ampEnv` (AnalogEnvelope): attack 0, decay 1230 ms, sustain 0, release 0.5 ms set at construction, then release 1.0 ms normal or 50 ms accented set per note (`rosic_Open303.cpp:43-47, 23-24`). So the gate-high level decays with tau 1.23 s (a slow, fixed decay; effectively a long gate-held tail), and the note-off release is nearly instant (1 ms) on normal notes, 50 ms on accented notes. Time constant mapping: `coeff = 1 - exp(-1/tau_samples)` (`rosic_AnalogEnvelope.cpp:88-89`).
- The VCA also receives the filter envelope: while the note is on, `ampEnvOut += 0.45*mainEnvOut + accentGain*4.0*mainEnvOut` (`rosic_Open303.h:380`). So on a normal note the filter envelope adds 0.45 of itself to the amp; on a full accent it adds 4.45 of itself, i.e. the accent is loud mainly through this term.

### 3.3 Accent

When the note has accent (`velocity >= 100` in Open303's MIDI mapping, `note->accent` from its sequencer):

- `accentGain = accent` (knob 0..100 % scaled to 0..1, `setAccent`: `accent = 0.01 * newAccent`), `setMainEnvDecay(accentDecay)` with `accentDecay = 200.0` ms fixed, ignoring the Decay knob; `ampEnv.setRelease(accentAmpRelease = 50.0)` (`rosic_Open303.cpp:228-233`). Normal note: `accentGain = 0`, decay = knob, release 1 ms.
- The extra cutoff term: `tmp2 = rc2(mainEnv)` where rc2 is a LeakyIntegrator with tau 15 ms (`rc2.setTimeConstant(15.0)`, line 56; `rc1.setTimeConstant(0.0)`, i.e. no smoothing on the main path). The cutoff gets `2^(accentGain * tmp2)`, so at full accent the sweep is up to one octave higher than the unaccented sweep, with a 15 ms rise (the spike) and then it follows the 200 ms decay. This is the "accent sweep" as Open303 models it.
- Extra level through `accentGain*4*mainEnv` (section 3.2).
- What Open303 does not model: the accent sweep circuit of the hardware charges a capacitor on each accented note, so the sweep builds when accented notes follow each other quickly and its size depends on the resonance setting (Whittle's analysis; MEM, UNVERIFIED, host not reachable). Open303's `rc2` is a 15 ms smoother only, with no inter-note memory and no resonance dependence; a1k0n's README says "no accent simulation at all". If P5 wants the build-up it is a design extension: a state `s` that on each accented trigger rises toward 1 by a fixed fraction and decays slowly between, adding `accentGain * s * (1 + k resonance)` octaves. Every number in that extension would be a prior (design choice), to be fitted by ear; I recommend building Open303's model first and adding the extension as an off-by-default internal switch.

### 3.4 Slide

- `Open303::setSlideTime`: `pitchSlewLimiter.setTimeConstant(0.2 * slideTime)` with `slideTime` in ms and default 60 (`rosic_Open303.cpp:16, 49, 132`; the source comment says "todo: tweak the scaling constant"). The slewer is a LeakyIntegrator on frequency in Hz (`rosic_Open303.h:360`, `y1 = in + coeff*(y1-in)`): an exponential glide in linear frequency with tau = 12 ms at the default. 90 % of the way takes about 28 ms, 99 % about 55 ms. (Linear frequency, not linear pitch: a big interval glides audibly faster at the start.) The VST exposes it as a separate parameter; the hardware has no slide-time knob (fixed). The nominal hardware figure is not confirmed (the only number seen: an Oakley service-note summary says "slide times can reach about one second", SEEN-SNIPPET, irrelevant to this unit). MEM, UNVERIFIED: the 303's slide is around 60 ms.
- Behaviour: `slideToNote` changes only `oscFreq` (target) and the accent state; it does not call `mainEnv.trigger()` or `ampEnv.noteOn`, and the previous note's gate is not released: in sequencer mode `if(note->slide && nextNote->gate) { noteOffCountDown = INT_MAX; slideToNextNote = true; }` (`rosic_Open303.h:29-33`). So: gate stays high into the next note, the filter envelope keeps decaying from where it was, the next note's pitch glides in. A slide flag on a step means a glide into the following step's note. The next note's accent state is applied (`slideToNote` sets `accentGain` and `mainEnv` decay for the new note, but does not retrigger).
- Gate length on normal notes: in sequencer mode a non-slide note is released after `getStepLengthInSamples()` samples (`rosic_Open303.h:36`), the full step. The amp release is 1 ms, so the audible note length is set by the gate, plus the 200 Hz de-click filter. The hardware's gate is shorter than the step (MEM, UNVERIFIED, roughly 50 to 60 %); in this engine the event's `dur` carries it, so the choice belongs to whoever sets `dur`.

## 6. DSP block list in order (Open303 `getSample`, `rosic_Open303.h:317-402`)

1. Note logic: trigger (filter envelope and amp envelope start, oscillator phase reset only when idle) or slide (target pitch and accent state change only).
2. Pitch slew: LeakyIntegrator on Hz, tau = 0.2 x slideTime ms (12 ms).
3. Oscillator at 4x oversampling: band-limited saw or tanh-square (negated).
4. Highpass 44.486 Hz (one pole).
5. Ladder filter `TB_303`: 4 coupled stages with the first stage coefficient doubled, feedback k through a 150 Hz one-pole highpass, coefficient b0(fx), k(fx), makeup g, output 2 g y4; per-sample cutoff `cutoff * 2^(tmp1 + tmp2)`.
6. Anti-alias filter, decimate to the base rate.
7. Allpass 14.008 Hz, highpass 24.167 Hz, notch 7.5164 Hz (bw 4.7), at the base rate.
8. VCA: `ampEnv` (tau 1230 ms) plus `(0.45 + 4 accentGain) x mainEnv` while the gate is on, through the 200 Hz de-click lowpass.
9. Level.

For P5 (architecture, not my decision): the ladder needs sample-level feedback, so it belongs in an AudioWorklet (or a whole-voice worklet) with envelopes and slide driven from the host via AudioParam automation; Open303 runs the oscillator and ladder at 4x (176.4 kHz) and the approximate coefficients are fitted for that rate (the `fx` polynomials are only valid for small normalised cutoffs; at 44.1 kHz and cutoffs near 10 kHz they would not be). Port the code under the MIT notice (THIRD_PARTY.md entry: Open303, Robin Schmidt, MIT, files `rosic_TeeBeeFilter.h`, `rosic_Open303.*`, `rosic_AnalogEnvelope.*`, `rosic_DecayEnvelope.*`, `rosic_MipMappedWaveTable.cpp`). Do not port a1k0n's code (no licence).

## 9. Anything wrong or missing in the frozen names

- `slideTime` is fine as a name, but note the hardware has no such knob; it is a nominal glide time (ms) with tau = 0.2 x slideTime. Keep it in the spec so the panel can leave it off the hardware face.
- `decay` should be documented in ms of tau (not to-1/10 as in the 808 spec), and `ev.accent` overrides it (accent notes always use 200 ms).
- No `tuning` vs `tune` problem (`tune` in cents is fine).
- Missing and optional: `accent` as a parameter is the knob; the per-note accent comes from `ev.accent`. The slide semantics need a ruling: on the 303 a slide flag on step n glides into step n+1 and holds the gate across the boundary (Open303 sequencer code, `rosic_Open303.h:29-33`). The brief says the step cell's `slide: true` reaches the instrument as `ev.slide`: P5 should treat `ev.slide` on a note as "this note's gate stays high and the next note, if it follows directly, glides in without retriggering"; the coordinator should confirm that this is the meaning the sequencer gives the flag (an alternative reading, "slide into this note", would be a one-step shift).
- I see no frozen name for a hidden accent-sweep build-up; if wanted it can stay an internal constant (section 3.3).
