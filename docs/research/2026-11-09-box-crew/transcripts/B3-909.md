## 1. What the engine already has (read)

`app/engine/synths.js` builds each type from `BUILD.<type> = {spec, build(inst)}`. A spec entry is `{min, max, value, unit}`. `makeInst` adds `level` and `drive`, an output gain `inst.out`, a voice bus `inst.pre`, and `panic`/`dispose`. `trigger(t, ev)` builds fresh nodes per hit through `voice(ctx, inst)` (`v.osc`, `v.noise`, `v.gain`, `v.filter`, `v.track`). Helpers: `setV`, `lin`, `expo`, `K.target`, `envDecay`, `series`, `vel(ev)`.

- `kick` already has `tune`, `punch` (start pitch), `sweep`, `tau`, `decay`, `hold`, `attack`, `click`, `clickDecay`, `clickLp`, `harmonic`, `drive`. A sine with an exponential pitch drop and a click. It is a one-sine voice.
- `clap` (flams of bandpassed noise plus a tail), `hat_closed`/`hat_open` (highpassed noise plus six squares), `shaker`, `perc` are 808/909-flavoured, tuned to one track.
- `sampler` (SEEN-CODE): `buffer, start, end, pitch (st), attack, release, loop, choke (group name), reverse, level`. Choke is a group name; a hit stops live voices of that group in any sampler on the same context with a 5 ms fade. `getBuffer(id)` comes from the Host. This is exactly what the 909 hats need.

## 2. Panel controls and ranges

Sources: Roland spec-sheet snippet (SEEN-SNIPPET, support.roland.com TR-909 Technical Specifications) and MusicRadar anatomy snippet (SEEN-SNIPPET). The two disagree on hats.

- Bass drum: Level, Tune, Decay, Attack (Roland snippet).
- Snare: Level, Tune, Tone, Snappy (Roland snippet).
- Toms (low, mid, high): Level, Tune, Decay (Roland snippet).
- Hi-hats: Roland snippet lists Level and Tune only; MusicRadar snippet says decay is adjustable on bass, toms, hats and cymbals. MEM (UNVERIFIED): the open hat has a Decay knob and the closed hat has Decay too on the panel. Treat hat decay as present and let the owner's reference settle it.
- Rim shot, hand clap: Level only (MEM, UNVERIFIED).
- Crash, ride: Level, Tune (Polynominal snippet, SEEN-SNIPPET).
- Accent: global accent level; on the snare it changes the noise-to-body balance (clone-manual snippet, SEEN-SNIPPET, Tiptop SD909). Not documented further in what I saw.
- Knob ranges (degrees, Hz, ms) are NOT published in anything I could read. Every numeric range in section 7 is a design choice, labelled so.

Control semantics seen in snippets:
- Snare: Tone emphasises the VCF filter and total decay; Snappy sets the amount of white noise (one third-party description). Tiptop SD909 manual: Tone sets the length of the noise, Snappy the gain of the noise. These two disagree on Tone; implement Tone as noise decay length plus a filter move, and let A9 decide against the reference.
- Bass drum: three envelopes. A sine oscillator plus a noise click; the pitch envelope's decay rate is not adjustable; the Attack control sets the overall pulse and noise level of the click; Decay sets the amplitude decay, and pitch shifts slightly with Decay (Waldorf manual and a French forum post, SEEN-SNIPPET via the first search; the Tiptop BD909 snippet agrees: "decay tune and the envelope attack and decay segments").

## 3. Analog voices: circuit facts

I found no primary schematic text. What I can state:

- Bass drum (S, search snippet): sine-ish oscillator, pitch set by an envelope plus Tune; click is a short impulse plus lowpass-filtered noise, summed and routed through its own envelope; a forum thread (electro-music.com, SEEN-SNIPPET) compares a 909-style BD schematic with a PCB and flags capacitor polarity errors and a missing 1K resistor, so any schematic found in the wild needs checking. Not a basis for numbers.
- Network 909 (network-909.de/circuit.htm) is the circuit analysis the ER-99 author cites (SEEN-PAGE: ER-99 README). I could not open it. A9 or a later pass should read it first for the oscillator frequencies and decay values.
- Snare: two oscillators plus filtered noise. MEM (UNVERIFIED): body oscillators near 180 Hz and 330 Hz, each with a short downward pitch glide, noise through a highpass; Tone and Snappy as above.
- Toms: pitched oscillator with a pitch envelope and a small noise component; three voices with different base pitches. MEM (UNVERIFIED): the toms are one circuit type at three tunings.
- Rim shot: a short resonant "ping" made of two or three fixed resonances, no noise. MEM. Corroborated by ER-99's three parallel bandpass filters (section 5).
- Hand clap: noise through a bandpass (around 1 kHz, MEM) with a multi-burst envelope (four short bursts roughly 10 ms apart, MEM) and a longer decay. ER-99 uses a 10 ms "delayConst" and five delayed stages (SEEN-CODE), which supports the burst spacing.
- Measurements (spectra, decay times, pitch glide traces): none found published in readable form. The verification plan below makes the owner's reference samples the measurement source.

## 4. Sampled voices

Facts (SEEN-SNIPPET, Polynominal and Attack Magazine as summarised by the search tool; MEM where noted):
- Closed hat, open hat, crash and ride are PCM samples, 6-bit coded. A forum poster recalled 8-bit; unverified.
- Sample rate 18 kHz is given by one page (Polynominal); another poster was unsure. Treat 18 kHz as likely, not confirmed.
- ROM: a 32 Kb Hitachi HN61256P (Polynominal); the open hat on IC69 is a forum claim.
- The open and closed hat share one channel circuit, so they cannot sound together: the closed hat chokes the open hat (Polynominal).
- Tune sets playback pitch for the cymbals and hats. Open-hat Decay sets how long the open sound rings (Polynominal).
- Recording source: Atsushi Hoshiai used a Paiste and Zildjian hi-hat pair and an 18-inch Paiste thin crash for crash and ride. MusicRadar says the ride was struck on the bell; Attack says the bow. They differ.
- ER-99 (SEEN-CODE) plays the hat with one source whose gain is an exponential ramp to 0.00001 over `decay_closed` 300 ms or `decay` 2000 ms, stops the previous source on each hit, and pitch is `playbackRate`.
- Character of 6-bit: audible quantisation noise, grainy decay tail, band-limit near 9 kHz at 18 kHz rate (derived). Reproduce by a bit-crush plus sample-rate reduction on a clean sample, as an option, not by default.

ER-99 code facts (SEEN-CODE, instruments.ts and generator.ts, read for design only; not copied):
- Bass drum: sine at 80 Hz base, decay 300 ms, pitch envelope amount 2.5x over 50 ms, noise tone 0.5 with 20 ms noise decay, lowpass 3 kHz, saturation 0.5.
- Snare: two-oscillator structure (osc, osc2) at 220 Hz base, pitch envelope 4x over 10 ms, decay 100 ms, noise tone 0.25 with 250 ms noise decay, a notch near 1 kHz.
- Toms: 100, 200, 300 Hz bases, offsets 100, -50, -80, pitch envelope 2x over 100 ms, decay 200 ms, small noise 0.05.
- Rim shot: three parallel bandpass filters at 220, 500, 950 Hz, Q 10.5, gains 10, 20, 30, decay 30 ms, highpass 100 Hz, saturation 3.
- Clap: noise through highpass 900 Hz then bandpass 1200 Hz, delayConst 10 ms spacing, 5 gain-decay stages, a lowpass 2200 Hz non-delayed noise tone decaying over 250 ms.
These are one author's hand-tuned approximations, not measurements of the 909. Use as starting points only.

## 6. Commercial recreations

- Roland Cloud TR-909 Software Rhythm Composer (SEEN-SNIPPET, roland.com features and Synthtopia): Analog Circuit Behavior modelling of the analog voices, plus the original sample ROM; a 16-step TR-REC with a separate lane per voice (mute, solo, shuffle, last step), 8 variations per pattern, weak hits, nine flam types, three sub-step types; per-instrument gain that overdrives the circuits; panning; TR-8S pre-mapped to volume, tune, decay. Lesson: per-voice drive and pan, and per-step weak hits and flams, are what the commercial version adds.
- D16 Drumazon (SEEN-SNIPPET, MusicRadar and Emusician reviews): each sound has at least three controls; kick, snare and toms get six knobs; extras named: Tune Depth and Decay for snare; Tone, Tune Decay and Tune Depth for toms; kick Tune Depth and Pitch; Decay for cymbals. Drumazon 2 adds a master compressor/limiter and a "standard 909 controls plus extras" mode (CDM snippet). I found no official parameter list. Lesson: the useful extension is exposing the pitch envelope (depth and decay) for kick and toms.
- AudioRealism ADM, Arturia's 909 kit: not found in my 7 searches. Unverified and not described.

## 8. Build specification for A7 (engine terms)

General: every voice is a `BUILD.<name>` with a `spec`, `trigger(t, ev)` using `voice()` and `vel(ev)`. Add types `tr909_bd, tr909_sd, tr909_lt, tr909_mt, tr909_ht, tr909_rs, tr909_cp`, and for hats/cymbals a sampler program (8.8) plus a synthesised fallback voice `tr909_hat_c, tr909_hat_o, tr909_ride, tr909_crash`. Prefix avoids changing existing `kick`, `clap`, `hat_*`, which the Guidance song uses. All specs include `level {0..1.5, 1}` and `drive {0..1}` (a `makeInst` default). Accent: `ev.acc` scales amplitude by 1.0 to 1.3 on all voices, and on the snare also raises the body-to-noise ratio. Ranges below are design choices (UNVERIFIED against hardware); defaults come from ER-99 (SEEN-CODE) and MEM.

8.1 tr909_bd. Path: sine osc -> body gain (amp env) -> sum; click = short noise through lowpass -> click gain (own env) -> sum; sum -> `inst.pre`.
- Pitch env: start `tune * (1 + depth)`, exponential to `tune` with time constant `pitchDecay`.
- Params: `tune` 40..90 Hz, def 55 (panel Tune). `decay` 0.08..0.9 s, def 0.3 (panel Decay). `attack` 0..1, def 0.5 (click level; panel Attack). `depth` 0..6, def 2.5 (extension, Drumazon-style; ER-99 2.5). `pitchDecay` 0.01..0.12 s, def 0.05 (ER-99 50 ms; hardware fixed, expose as extension). `clickDecay` 0.002..0.03 s, def 0.012. `clickLp` 500..8000 Hz, def 3000 (ER-99 3 kHz). `level`, `drive` def 0.35.
- Amplitude: attack 1 ms, exponential decay to -80 dB over `decay`. Pitch drift with decay: add `tune * (1 + 0.04 * decayNorm)` is OPTIONAL; skip unless A9 shows it.

8.2 tr909_sd. Path: two sine/triangle oscs (body) with a short downward pitch glide -> body gain; white noise -> highpass -> optional notch -> noise gain (snappy); sum -> `inst.pre`.
- Params: `tune` 120..300 Hz, def 190 (osc 1 base; osc 2 = tune * 1.83, ratio MEM from 180/330 UNVERIFIED, ER-99 220 base). `tone` 0..1, def 0.5 (noise decay 0.08..0.35 s and highpass 1500..4500 Hz both scale with tone). `snappy` 0..1, def 0.5 (noise gain). `bodyDecay` 0.04..0.3 s, def 0.1 (ER-99 100 ms). `glide` 0..4, def 1.0 (pitch multiplier at start; ER-99 4x over 10 ms). `glideTime` 0.003..0.03 s, def 0.01. `level`, `drive` def 0.3.
- Accent raises the noise-to-body ratio by up to +3 dB.

8.3 tr909_lt, tr909_mt, tr909_ht. One BUILD factory called thrice. Path: sine osc with pitch env -> amp env; a little noise (0.05) for the attack transient -> `inst.pre`.
- Params: `tune` (lt 70..160 Hz def 100; mt 110..260 def 160; ht 160..400 def 240; bases from ER-99 100/200/300 adjusted, UNVERIFIED), `decay` 0.1..0.8 s def 0.3 (ER-99 200 ms), `depth` 0..3 def 1.0 (ER-99 2x = depth 1), `pitchDecay` 0.02..0.25 s def 0.1 (ER-99 100 ms), `noise` 0..0.3 def 0.05. `level`, `drive` def 0.25.

8.4 tr909_rs. Path: short click (1 ms noise burst or 50 us impulse) -> three parallel bandpass filters at `f1`, `f2`, `f3` -> gains -> highpass 100 Hz -> saturation -> `inst.pre`.
- Params: `tune` 0.5..2, def 1 (multiplies f1..f3 = 220, 500, 950 Hz; ER-99 values, Q 10.5, gains 10:20:30), `decay` 0.01..0.08 s def 0.03. `drive` def 0.6. `level`.

8.5 tr909_cp. Path: noise -> bandpass (centre `tone`, Q 0.7..2) -> burst env -> `inst.pre`; bursts: 3 or 4 spaced `spread`, then a tail with decay `decay`.
- Params: `tone` 700..2500 Hz def 1200 (ER-99 900 HP + 1200 BP, MEM ~1 kHz), `spread` 0.006..0.02 s def 0.01 (ER-99 10 ms), `bursts` 2..5 def 4, `decay` 0.1..0.6 s def 0.25, `tail` 0..1 def 0.4. Reuse the existing `clap` envelope code with these defaults if cheaper; the new type exists so the Guidance clap does not change.

8.6 Choke and shared channel. Closed hat chokes open hat: sampler `choke: 'tr909_hat'` on both (existing mechanism, 5 ms fade).

8.7 Sampler program the batch-1 sampler would load (the sampled voices). One `sampler` track (or four) per voice, with the owner's samples:
| voice | sampler params |
|---|---|
| hat closed | `buffer` = id of closed-hat sample, `pitch` 0 (panel Tune maps -6..+6 st), `attack` 0.0005, `release` 0.02, `loop` 0, `choke` 'tr909_hat', `level` 1 |
| hat open | `buffer` = open-hat sample, `pitch` 0, `attack` 0.0005, `release` 0.3 (maps open Decay: gate length via `end` or release 0.05..1.5 s), `loop` 0, `choke` 'tr909_hat', `level` 1 |
| ride | `buffer`, `pitch` 0, `release` 0.5, `loop` 0, `choke` '' |
| crash | `buffer`, `pitch` 0, `release` 0.5, `loop` 0, `choke` '' |
Note: the sampler release applies at the end of the slice, not as a free decay; for a tuneable open hat, either set `end` to cut the slice (decay control = `end` fraction) or add a `tr909_hatEnv` gain stage. Recommendation: add a `decay` param (s) to the `sampler` as an optional exponential amplitude fall; that matches ER-99 and the hardware's decay knob. That is a B1/A7 coordination point.
Panel Tune maps to `pitch` in semitones; hardware range unverified.

8.8 Synthesised fallbacks (so the kit ships without samples). The existing `hatBuild` with a 6-square bank (R ratios) is the 808-style metallic source. For 909 hats use the same bank at 18 kHz-style band-limit: highpass 6 kHz, lowpass 9 kHz, then an optional bit-crush (a WaveShaper quantised to 64 levels; sample-rate reduction is not available natively, so use a 18 kHz offline-rendered buffer). Best route: render the fallback once at load into an AudioBuffer through an OfflineAudioContext and register it as an internal sampler buffer. Params: `closedDecay` 0.02..0.2 s def 0.055, `openDecay` 0.1..1.5 s def 0.35, `tune` -6..6 st, `crush` 0..1 def 0.5 (bit depth 8 -> 6, rate 44.1 -> 18 kHz). Ride and crash: the same noise-plus-bank with longer decay (ride 1.2 s, crash 2 s; ER-99 decay 2000 ms) and lowpass 12 kHz.

8.9 Tests A7 owes: add each new type to the stubs in `selfcheck.js` (new node types need matching stubs), keep `Synths.types()` listing them, and no new node type beyond oscillator, buffer source, biquad, gain, waveshaper.

## 9. Verification plan for A9 (against the owner's reference samples)

Inputs from the owner: one dry hit per voice from a TR-909 (hardware or Roland Cloud), at default panel settings, then BD at three Tune/Decay settings, SD at three Tone/Snappy settings, toms at default, hats open at minimum/maximum Decay. Record the sample rate and bit depth.

1. Render each new voice through `tools/render.js` (one hit, level 1, no drive, velocity 1) at 44.1 kHz; trim leading silence to the first sample above -50 dBFS on both.
2. Per voice, score (use or extend `tools/score.py`; otherwise a small Python script outside the repo):
   - Time: peak position, time to -20/-40/-60 dB, RMS over 0-10 ms, 10-50 ms, 50-200 ms. Pass: each decay time within 15%.
   - Spectrum: log-spaced band energies (1/3-octave, 40 Hz to 16 kHz) of the first 80 ms and of the tail; spectral centroid. Pass: band energy differences under 3 dB below 4 kHz, under 6 dB above (noise-dominated).
   - Pitch (BD, toms, SD body): fundamental track via short-window autocorrelation or FFT peak per 5 ms; start frequency, end frequency, glide time (to within 15%). Compare with the `tune`, `depth`, `pitchDecay` values fitted.
   - Click (BD): level of the first 3 ms relative to the body peak (dB).
   - Clap: burst count and spacing from the envelope (onset detector, 1 ms hop); expect 4 bursts near 10 ms, then a tail.
   - Rim: resonance peak frequencies (expect three near 220, 500, 950 Hz scaled by the reference).
   - Hats and cymbals: loudness-normalised waveform difference only when the owner's sample is loaded into the sampler; for the synthesised fallback use spectrum, decay time and the 18 kHz band limit (energy above 9 kHz at least 30 dB down).
3. Fit procedure: for each voice, adjust only the spec defaults in the section 8 order (decay, tune, depth/pitchDecay, tone) until the metrics pass; record the final defaults. No metric passes by changing the metric.
4. Parameter sweeps: render BD `decay` at min, mid, max and check monotone decay times; SD `snappy` 0, 0.5, 1 and check noise band energy grows; HH open `decay` the same. Check ranges against the owner's panel-sweep samples if provided.
5. Choke: sequence open hat then closed hat 120 ms later with the same choke group; the open hat's energy after the closed-hat onset must fall below -60 dB within 20 ms.
6. Headroom and sanity: peak below 0 dBFS at level 1 and velocity 1; no NaN; `selfcheck.js` passes; a hit while a previous hit sounds does not click (cut with `panic`).
7. Report: a table of metric, reference, rendered, pass/fail per voice. Failures that would need a model change (for example a two-stage pitch envelope on the toms) go back to A7 with the plot.
